01Why this matters

The card works.
The experience doesn't.

1.2M cards issued, but a third churn every year — lost to friction, not to the product. Three goals define the fix.

Activation1–2 yr
50%→70%
Turn dormant new cards into active ones by fixing onboarding & first use.
Annual churn3–4 yr
33.5%↓24%
Better servicing & transparency remove the reasons people disengage.
Purchase-active accountsongoing
600k→1M
Deepen the retail flywheel — more frequency, more Walmart-linked spend.

03The existing experience

Three systems.
Zero coherence.

Not broken — just scattered across three surfaces with three logins that never talk to each other.

🧩
Fragmented onboarding
Customers hop across systems with repeated identity checks. Every hop loses people before the card is ever activated.
Drop-off before activation
🙈
Invisible basics
Balance, rewards, and due dates live behind a separate login — so simple questions become support calls.
High support volume
🔒
No self-service
Setting up auto-pay once meant printing a form and visiting a branch. No reminders, no safety nets.
Missed actions & frustration

✅
Works well
Core tasks completed with ease
Application, activation, payment, rewards — all cleared without hesitation. The single-screen payment drew special praise.
Next: finalise & ship current flows as designed.
🧠
Mental model confirmed
Users expect Walmart to own card tasks
They assumed everything lived in the Walmart app, not a partner site — validating the Hub's core premise.
Next: keep core tasks native; hand off to Fairstone invisibly.
🧭
Gap found
Discoverability: they looked elsewhere
Users expected the card & rewards under Accounts, Wallet, or Savings — 27% missed it on first click.
Next: add entry points in Accounts, Wallet & Savings.
💭
Gap found
"Total balance" confused users
It was read as the available credit limit — some risked paying the wrong amount, some lost trust.
Next: add an info icon & reword with content partners.
