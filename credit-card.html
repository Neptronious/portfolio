03 The Existing Experience

Three systems.
Zero coherence.

Not broken — just scattered across three surfaces with three logins that never talk to each other.

Challenge 01
Fragmented onboarding
Customers hop across systems with repeated identity checks. Every hop loses people before the card is ever activated.
Drop-off before activation
Challenge 02
Invisible basics
Balance, rewards, and due dates live behind a separate login — so simple questions become support calls.
High support volume
Challenge 03
No self-service
Setting up auto-pay once meant printing a form and visiting a branch. No reminders, no safety nets. There was no option to make a bill payment online at all.
Missed actions & frustration
↳

The issuing bank had plans to build a fully operational credit card management application — but without a timeline or a roadmap, it remained firmly in the future. Customers couldn't wait that long. Rather than hold on for an uncertain delivery, we made the call to build it ourselves: bringing the card experience into the Avenue Mart app now, for the people who needed it today.

05 The Systems Decision

Avenue Mart owns the feel. Fairstone owns the bank.

Rebuilding regulated banking flows natively would have added a year of legal and compliance work. Instead, we drew a clear line: high-frequency customer moments live in our UI, while regulated workflows run inside the issuing bank's secure webview — invisible to the user, but handled correctly.

Native · Avenue Mart

Everything a customer touches weekly

High-frequency, retail-connected moments live in our interface — where we control the experience, the analytics, and the link back to shopping.

DashboardBalancesRewardsPaymentsTransactions
Embedded · Issuing bank

Everything a regulator touches

Sensitive and regulated workflows stay inside the issuer's secure webview, reached silently through single sign-on so it never feels like leaving.

Gap found
Users had a challenge in locating the credit card on the app.
Users expected the card & rewards under Accounts, Wallet, or Savings — 27% missed it on first click.
Next: add entry points in Accounts, Wallet & Savings.
Gap found
"Total balance" confused users
It was read as the available credit limit — some risked paying the wrong amount, some lost trust.
Next: Change copy to "current balance," which is commonly understood, and add an info icon for support.
Nobody went looking for a "credit card" option. They went looking for Account, and Wallet.
